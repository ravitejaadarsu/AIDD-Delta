# Commercial strategy — hypotheses to validate

This is a proposed founder plan, not a revenue forecast, customer claim or launched paid plan.
The existing repository license is unchanged. No payment service, paid subscription, outreach
campaign or external account has been created by these changes.

## Positioning

Proposed promise: **know what was verified, against which source, before shipping AI-assisted
changes**. Lead with the working stale-evidence demo and an example report. Avoid positioning
agent count, lengthy protocols or unmeasured superiority as the reason to buy.

The competitive reference points already offer substantial workflows:
[Superpowers](https://github.com/obra/superpowers) documents design-to-review flows and
commercial support; [AI-DLC](https://github.com/awslabs/aidlc-workflows) documents an engine,
approvals and multiple runtime integrations. The hypothesis is that a focused evidence
workflow can fit alongside an existing agent setup and solve a narrower buyer problem.
Sources reviewed September 24, 2026; this is positioning inference, not market-size research.

## First buyer hypothesis

Start with a small engineering team already shipping AI-assisted changes into an existing
codebase, with an engineering lead who personally reviews the results. Qualify for a concrete
pain: outdated test proof, missing requirements, expensive manual verification, or inconsistent
handoffs. Avoid selling compliance assurance before authenticated approvals and independent
controls exist. Choose one segment after actual interviews; “every developer” is too broad
for an initial offer.

## Offer experiments

| Offer | Scope | Proposed price test, not a published commitment | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assisted evaluation | One repo, setup, 5–10 changes, findings report | Test a fixed USD 500–1,500 pilot | Buyer agrees to pay for a defined outcome and delivery effort fits the fee |
| Team enablement | Templates, workflow integration, training and handover | Quote after measuring pilot effort | Team can maintain it without daily founder intervention |
| Ongoing support | Agreed response window and periodic workflow review | Test USD 200–500/month after a successful pilot | Repeated need and support load justify recurring billing |
| Managed evidence workspace | Authenticated approvals, history, policy and integrations | Do not set pricing yet | Multiple paying pilots independently request the same hosted capability |

These are interview anchors, not validated willingness to pay. Track founder hours and direct
service costs per pilot. Stop or narrow an offer whose delivery economics do not work. Do not
promise fixed defect reductions or publish invented ROI.

## Next 30 days

1. Polish the first-run demo and publish the exact scope/limitations of the evidence checker.
2. Ask five qualified engineering leads to show their current review process. Obtain permission
   before inspecting code or recording calls; do not gather proprietary data in public issues.
3. Offer two narrowly scoped pilots with written acceptance criteria and an explicit fee.
4. Measure installation time, checks captured, stale/missing proof detected, false blockers,
   reviewer time, execution overhead and whether the team continues using it.
5. Publish a permissioned case study, including failures. Choose the next product feature from
   repeated paid demand rather than broad feature requests.

The repository now includes a pilot inquiry form and [team evaluation guide](teams.md).
Those are intake materials; they do not substitute for customer conversations or sales.

## What to keep open and what could be paid

Keep receipt format, local capture/check, basic reporting and reproducible fixtures accessible
in the core. Candidate paid value is operational work: setup/support, integrations, authenticated
team approvals, managed retention and organization administration. Build the service only after
validating the need. A business model based solely on access to prompt text is easy to replace.

[GitHub Sponsors](https://docs.github.com/en/sponsors/getting-started-with-github-sponsors/about-github-sponsors)
is another possible support channel; eligibility, enrollment and payment setup belong to the
owner. No Sponsors enrollment or funding link has been assumed or configured here.

## Product work still required before a hosted offer

Authentication/authorization, tenant isolation, encrypted storage, retention/deletion controls,
secrets policy, trusted execution/attestation, support operations, billing and a clear service
agreement. Local unsigned receipts must never be marketed as an independent security or
compliance attestation. Validate market demand before taking on this operating burden.
